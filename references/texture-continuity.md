# Preserve the sound the listener is following

Read when a transition keeps the beat/level but still feels abruptly disconnected, or when a synth, pad, vocal space or recognizable motif disappears at the handoff. For a set-wide complaint, inspect every adjacency; a cited pair is an example rather than the scope of the repair.

## Map the audible roles over time

Track the foreground statement, tonal motif/texture, groove, bass owner and spatial tail across departure, preparation, full entry and completion. Ask what draws attention before the boundary, whether it resolves naturally, which incoming element inherits that attention, and what happens to its decay. A band of similar energy in the next recording does not continue the previous sound's identity. A de-click fade of a few milliseconds prevents an electrical discontinuity but does not finish a musical gesture.

Inspect the actual selected recording in playback time, with enough context on both sides and any proposed continuation beyond the cut. Do not infer motif completion from BPM, RMS, spectral centroid, key labels or ASR words alone. A clear lyrical endpoint can still have an unresolved instrumental lead.

## Select the treatment per pair

- **Natural continuation:** retain a bounded outgoing instrumental estimate beyond the original cut when it finishes the gesture and can coexist with the incoming statement. Reduce its level deliberately as the new sound takes ownership; avoid repeatedly restarting the motif.
- **Prepared release:** start reducing the specific outgoing texture before the cut, retaining the groove/foreground statement. A short spatial decay can cross the handoff even when the pitched material should stop earlier. Reverb/delay should inherit the outgoing sound rather than add unrelated noise.
- **Incoming introduction:** tease a selected non-vocal incoming motif while the old one releases, then restore its full statement. Record whether this is a repeated teaser or a continuous source pickup. Avoid strong concurrent melodies unless their interaction has actually been auditioned.
- **Explicit pivot:** allow a rhythmic change while giving the old sound a clear ending. For incompatible pulse runs, favor a short non-rhythmic decay over an extended old drum/arp loop.
- **Leave intact:** a resolved gesture or intentional cut can already work. Audit coverage is not a reason to add an effect everywhere.

For vocal interludes, also account for the backing texture. Keeping only drums/bass under a held voice can remove an important pad/synth abruptly, and postponing all incoming melody until the full recording can create a second abrupt reveal.

Treat separated stems as estimates. A source-minus-vocals/drums/bass residual includes reconstruction error and may contain leaked voices/percussion. It is not a verified isolated synth. Use the original source's joint scale, preserve the bass owner and keep proposed melodic overlaps bounded. Rendered master normalization can change levels; compare near-boundary material at similar loudness.

## Keep edits aligned to the actual recording

Independent atempo runs starting at different offsets can have different local waveform timing. Do not subtract a retimed stem from a prepared recording merely because their sample rates match. Separate bounded probes in the actual prepared playback domain, retain source-frame mappings, or verify alignment before making complementary edits. A source with dynamic normalization needs local scale/alignment checks; a continuation may require a different technique when those checks fail.

Record source slices, layer roles, gain envelopes, continuation/repeat/FX parameters, and why a treatment was chosen. Make altered media independent of the previous delivery so A/B still refers to two real candidates. If adding a tail, verify its ending too: moving the cliff a second later has not solved the problem.

## Audition and evidence

Use the same pair and cue context for A/B. Provide the complete set and every requested adjacency, with departure/arrival markers and enough context after the last tail. Band activity and tonal similarity can prioritize review; they cannot certify sound identity, harmonic fit or perceived smoothness. If audio perception is unavailable, keep those judgments unverified and label the result an audition candidate.

These describe context-dependent use of stems, EQ and spatial FX; they do not establish the cue choices or guarantee the quality of a new set.
