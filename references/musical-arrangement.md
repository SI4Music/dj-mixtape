# Play a relationship between recordings

Read when a set feels like successive songs with extra bass/FX, or its melodies remain disconnected after fades and tails have been improved. Reconsider the recording pair, selected sections and shared musical idea before designing another repair layer.

## Select a relationship with an audible payoff

Describe what the listener follows in A, what B answers, and where that answer becomes recognizable. Useful proposals include a shared riff or note contour, contrasting deliveries by the same voice, a recurring timbre, a complete lyrical call/response, and a pulse reinterpretation. Artist/title relationships can suggest a scene; they do not prove that two audio sections tell a lyrical story. Select the relevant hook, verse, break or pickup rather than automatically taking nearly every song to its last downbeat.

Local key/chroma candidates help retrieve sections. Check the actual motif notes, chord changes, bass and pitched voice in the proposed overlap. A whole-song key label can conceal a different section; a strong chroma match can still join unrelated phrases. Treat note trackers on a polyphonic stem as uncertain transcriptions. Choose a simpler bridge or another section when the isolated material is weak.

Practitioner context: [Erol Alkan, Flava D, Butch and DJ Puffy interviewed by Pioneer DJ](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/how-do-djs-approach-harmonic-mixing/) describe complementary, ear-led uses of harmonic mixing. Their advice supports testing musical relationships; it does not verify a model's key or cue prediction.

## Choose a routine that changes the arrangement

- **Continuous instrumental introduction:** run B's selected pre-cue material through its full entry without restarting the phrase. Remove A's melody progressively while its complete foreground statement finishes. Introduce B's melody in that space, then its lead. Give the low end one owner; avoid two basslines during a long lead overlap. Record which B vocals were suppressed and why the full-entry cue is a complete statement.
- **Tone-play:** select a stable pitched sound from A and use it to play B's specific note contour/rhythm, then hand over to B's original motif. Record the source sample, reference pitch, target note events, transpositions and payoff cue. Pitching an entire chord, an octave-error transcription or a generic oscillator to a key is not verified tone-play. Large shifts or leaked voices may make a proposal unusable.
- **Cue juggling / call-response:** alternate selected source hits or short statements in a deliberate rhythmic pattern. Leave space for the answer. Do not chop a whole verse into meaningless syllables solely to demonstrate an effect; preserve intelligibility when lyrics are the connection.
- **Pulse reinterpretation:** establish a new groove through source-derived subdivisions or a short polyrhythmic gesture. Write the pulse ratio and shared duration explicitly. A numerical 3:2 or half-time relationship does not establish compatible accents or musical phrasing.
- **Source-derived FX punctuation:** reverse a recognizable hit into its forward attack, tighten a short source loop, apply a brake to an instrumental gesture, or make a brief filtered/granular response. Identify its setup, event and release. Use only a few signature moments when that suits the set; constant FX can obscure the music.

[Crossfader's transition walkthrough](https://blog.wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/12-high-energy-dj-transitions/) documents tone-play, loop cutting, pitch play and brake routines. These descriptions informed the technique choices above; a written tutorial is distinct from a performance actually heard. [Pioneer DJ's practitioner tips](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/8-tips-and-tricks-for-all-types-of-djs/) include Eats Everything's short-loop transformations, Andrea Oliva's vocal loops and Kenny Allstar's contextual signature FX. These are context-dependent examples, not a requirement to add an effect at every handoff.

## Build a controllable musical proposal

Separate bounded material in its actual playback domain. If subtracting parts, use complementary, frame-aligned stems with their original joint scale; verify the reconstruction instead of independently retiming parts and assuming alignment. Retain voice, rhythm, bass, melody and performed gesture as separately editable items around the transition. Keep the original master recordings for the rest of the set.

Distinguish a continuous pickup from a repeated teaser, and an offline varispeed gesture from live scratching. Export isolated routines and enough preceding/following context to assess the payoff, alongside the complete set. When sequencing changes, label V5/V6 excerpts as arrangement comparisons; they are not a controlled A/B of one FX.

Check note/sample mappings, source and timeline bounds, bass ownership, voice overlap, gesture endings, layer sums and current export hashes. These establish that the intended arrangement was rendered. Perceived melody, voice energy, smoothness and stem leakage still require listening. If perception is unavailable, deliver an audition candidate and leave musical judgments unverified.
