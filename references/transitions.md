# Transition design

Start with [transition-thinking.md](transition-thinking.md) when designing musical flow or diagnosing lost momentum. Define the entry, preparation and payoff for each pair before selecting an effect.

When fades and effect beds still leave unrelated musical statements, use [musical-arrangement.md](musical-arrangement.md) to choose sections, transfer melodic ownership and construct expressive source-derived routines.

For an unresolved synth/pad/motif or abrupt loss of space, use [texture-continuity.md](texture-continuity.md). Give the outgoing sound an ending and the incoming sound an introduction; equivalent band energy is not continuity of identity.

For a mismatch in singer/rapper delivery, use [vocal-interludes.md](vocal-interludes.md). Beat continuity alone does not preserve vocal intensity. An interlude needs a lead trajectory, a groove role and a specific incoming statement.

Decide per adjacency: phrase cut, short drum blend, filtered loop bridge, echo-out into a new downbeat, or half/double-time switch. Scratching is optional; do not call a simple sound effect a genuine scratch routine.

For blends, align selected phrases and protect one intelligible lead. Prefer low-vocal/instrumental cues over two verses at full volume. Trade bass ownership rather than summing two 808s throughout. Specify beats/bars and convert using the playback tempo.

Large tempo jumps can use a deliberate phrase cut rather than aggressive stretching. Echo-outs need bounded feedback, filtered return and explicit tail/entry cue. Loop bridges require selected source slice, repeat count and source offset recorded.

Incoming percussion can prepare a handoff beneath the outgoing phrase rather than occupying a separate beatless gap. A thin solo drum bridge or cold source intro can lower momentum. Keep the outgoing bass/groove until the incoming full-beat cue unless a deliberate breakdown has a clear payoff. Source context, stems and phrase/vocal review should inform the choice; do not choose transition types by track index.

Use meaningful gain/filter control points. Equal-power fades can still produce excess low-end. Check rendered transition audio, not just envelope shapes. Avoid cutting recognizable words and delaying hooks without purpose.

Reference: [Pioneer DJ genre mixing techniques](https://blog.pioneerdj.com/djtips/we-uncover-the-mixing-techniques-behind-every-major-genre/).
