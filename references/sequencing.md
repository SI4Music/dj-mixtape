# Set sequencing

Translate the user's energy preference into blocks. Artist variety, recognizable hooks, bass density and vocal texture can matter more than increasing BPM throughout. An 808 does not establish drill; label actual genre conservatively.

Audible duration is `sum(source segment length/playback rate) - sum(overlaps) + inserted bridges`. Compute it from timeline item positions rather than catalog durations. Native-speed segments are valid for deliberate phrase cuts.

Use modest pitch-preserving stretching in compatible tempo blocks, and intentional cut/echo resets for large differences. BPM/key compatibility does not prove musical compatibility. Keep source cuts, output positions, rates, overlaps and selection reasons explicit.

If the user specifies a track count, curate that many recordings and choose their entries and exits from the arrangement. Calculate the duration afterward. Never allocate equal seconds per track, choose a long closer to absorb a remainder, or cut the final recording at an arithmetic deadline. Removing a duration constraint removes all of those compensations.

Give the opener an intentional introduction or pickup into its first musical statement. Give the closer a resolved final section and its natural audible tail. For internal handoffs, use source structure, lead-phrase completion, rhythmic cues and the incoming recording together; a detected downbeat or an integer number of bars alone does not establish a suitable exit. Document model-assisted section estimates separately from listening-confirmed vocal or hook semantics.

Only enforce an exact duration when the user explicitly retains that requirement. Meet it through arrangement choices that preserve the opening and ending; do not pad with unexplained silence, duplicate tracks or globally speed the whole set to hide a duration error.
